How LeadHunt was built to fix broken lead generation

From a slow manual process to a smart Chrome Extension that scrapes, classifies, and pitches in minutes.

The Problem

Lead generation was broken for agencies

Agency founders and sales teams were spending 3 to 4 hours every day doing the same manual work. Search Google Maps. Copy a business name. Find a phone number. Open a new tab to search for an email. Write a pitch from scratch. Repeat 50 times.

The tools that existed were either expensive SaaS products with monthly subscriptions, or crude scrapers that got blocked immediately or returned only 10% of the actual results.

Headless browser scrapers are detected and blocked by Google almost immediately. They also miss most results due to virtual scrolling.

SaaS tools charge per lead or per month. For an agency doing daily prospecting, costs add up to thousands of rupees a month.
The Insight

The browser is the best scraper

Google Maps cannot distinguish between a real user and a content script running inside Chrome. A Chrome Extension uses the exact same session, cookies, IP address, and user agent as normal browsing. No blocking, no CAPTCHAs, no proxies needed.

The only technical challenge was virtual scrolling. Google Maps removes cards from the DOM as you scroll. Every existing solution tried to collect data after scrolling and captured almost nothing. LeadHunt reads card data during the scroll, the moment each card appears.

The winning insight was that every card that briefly exists in the DOM can be read, even if it gets removed a second later. Harvest during the scroll, not after.
Core architecture principle
The Solution

Two-phase scraping architecture

LeadHunt solves the virtual DOM problem with a two-phase approach that captures complete data from every listing regardless of scroll position.

1
Phase 1 — Harvest during scroll
As the results panel scrolls, a MutationObserver continuously reads every card that enters the viewport. Name, category, rating, reviews, phone, address, and Maps URL are captured into a deduplicated Map. By the end of scrolling, all leads are stored regardless of DOM state.
2
Phase 2 — Enrich only what is missing
Only listings that are missing a website or phone number get individually opened for enrichment. Most leads are already complete from Phase 1, so this step is minimal. This keeps the total scrape time fast while still getting deep data where it matters.
+
Email extraction
For each listing with a website, the extension fetches the homepage HTML and extracts email addresses using a regex with a blacklist that filters out platform emails, CDN addresses, and noreply addresses. Domain-matching emails are prioritised.

The Pitch Model

Personalised outreach without an AI subscription

The pitch model is a rule-based classifier built into the extension. It runs entirely offline and generates a tailored email or WhatsApp message in under 50 milliseconds. No API calls, no subscription, no latency.

For each lead it reads the business category, classifies it into one of 19 segments, identifies the most likely pain point for that segment, selects the 3 most relevant services to pitch, and adapts the opening hook based on the lead's data signals.

Design

Warm, precise, and intentional

The design avoids the cold blue-grey palette that most developer tools default to. A warm sand background with a red-orange accent creates a tool that feels built for sales people, not engineers.

Why Plus Jakarta Sans?
Strong character at display weight (800) while staying legible at 10px label size. Generic system fonts lose personality at small sizes.
Why Lucide icons?
Inline SVGs render identically on every OS and Chrome version. They inherit color via currentColor, eliminating separate icon color variables.
Why warm neutrals?
Cold greys feel like system UI. Warm sand and charcoal feel like a product someone chose to design, not a default.
Why no em-dashes?
They look typographically heavy in tight UI copy at small sizes and break awkwardly in narrow containers. Colons and commas are cleaner.
